BUSINESS DEVELOPMENT · IT

An agentic business framework, for any business.

Most business software welds three things together: the data a business holds, the intelligence about what should happen next, and the tools that carry that out. This framework separates them — so it isn't a logistics product, or a Dutch product, or a product tied to any one industry. It's an architecture any organisation can run its own data and rules through.

Layer 1
Data
A schema-driven model of the business's own entities, wherever they already live — ERP, CRM, spreadsheets, legacy systems. No forced migration to someone else's data shape.
Layer 2
Process intelligence
Agentic reasoning over that data — understanding what should happen next against the business's own rules, not a vendor's assumptions about how the business works.
Layer 3
Execution tooling
Connectors that carry decisions into whatever systems already run the business, instead of replacing them.

Because the three layers are decoupled, the same framework runs in logistics or professional services: swap the data model and the connectors, keep the reasoning layer.

Your questions are your edge

Data has become a commodity. What sets organisations apart is the questions they ask of it: which ones, how precisely, how consistently. The framework makes those questions explicit and keeps them independent of where the data lives.

Where else this applies

Illustrative, not client work — the same three-layer architecture, a different data model and connectors each time:

Production
Production and quality data driving automated scheduling and monitoring decisions.
Maritime services
Towage, mooring, pilotage and crew-transport data driving scheduling and coordination across a vessel's port call.
Sports video & data intelligence
Performance and video data analysis of individual athletes, for sporting teams.
Public-sector case management
Case and compliance data driving routing and follow-up across departments.
